    28. Platform and software¶

    Depth: L2 · Reading time: ~10 min · Last verified: 2026-11-01 Prerequisites: Control planes and fleet management · Consumption models · Purity and DirectFlash · FlashArray and FlashBlade

    TL;DR¶

    • One software stack, many kinds of hardware. Purity runs on every array, Fusion and Pure1 manage the fleet, Portworx and Everpure Cloud extend it to Kubernetes and public cloud, and Data Intelligence (ex-1touch) reads what the data is. Umbrella names: Enterprise Data Cloud (2025), "Data Primacy" (2026).
    • Evergreen is the business model that makes the platform work. Customers subscribe to an architecture; controllers and media are refreshed in place with no data migration. Subscription ARR reached $2.13B (+20%) in Q2 FY271.
    • Switching costs now live in the control plane. Fusion presets and automation outlast any hardware refresh. Over 1,200 customers used Fusion by Q1 FY27, double the prior quarter2, still under a tenth of the base.
    • 1touch ($125.0M, closed 2026-05-07)3 bets that storage metadata plus content classification becomes a governance and AI-readiness product. It is small next to Portworx (~$353M, 2020)4 but enters categories owned by data catalogs and data-security vendors.
    • Every incumbent is making the same pitch. Dell, NetApp, HPE and VAST all sell a "data platform"; the differences are what sits beneath (one OS or a portfolio), reach beyond own hardware, and whether customers buy the top layers.

    Why this matters for Everpure strategy¶

    Hardware margins ride the NAND cycle; Everpure raised prices in February 2026 as component costs surged (Chapter 29). Subscription gross margin was 74.9% in Q2 FY27 against 66.2% for product (both non-GAAP)5. The platform aims to make revenue compound through attach and expansion. Subscription net dollar retention was 113% in FY266, so a cohort paying $100 one year paid $113 the next.

    The risk: if Fusion, Portworx and Data Intelligence mostly ship inside array deals rather than winning standalone budgets, "data management company" is a rebrand on a storage P&L. The options a strategy team might evaluate, based on public information, turn on that distinction.

    Figure 28.1 — One OS on every array, one control plane over every array, and the newest layers (Portworx, cloud, data intelligence) plugged into that control plane rather than into individual boxes.

    The bottom three layers are covered in Chapter 26, Chapter 27 and Chapter 22. The layers above need no Everpure hardware: Portworx serves Kubernetes anywhere, Everpure Cloud runs Purity in AWS and Azure, and Data Intelligence classifies data wherever it lives.

    Semiconductor bridge: this is Synopsys and Cadence moving from point tools to flows. A simulator is easy to swap; a sign-off flow with years of scripts and qualified recipes is not. Fusion presets and automation are Everpure's "flow"; the arrays are the tools.

    Naming has shifted, so be precise. The Enterprise Data Cloud (June 2025) was a unified on-premises-plus-cloud control plane with Fusion at its core7; the CEO tied the February 2026 Everpure name to it and to Evergreen8. Counsel and filings now say "Everpure Platform"9. At Accelerate 2026 the framing became Data Primacy, trusted data rather than applications at the centre10, with pillars of data management, a unified data plane and an intelligent control plane11. "Pure Storage Cloud" is now Everpure Cloud12.

    Evergreen: subscribing to an architecture¶

    Evergreen began in 2015; Evergreen Gold became Evergreen//Forever in the 2022 restructuring13 that produced today's three tiers14.

    Tier Who owns the hardware What you pay for Refresh mechanics Key guarantees (company-stated)
    Evergreen//Forever Customer Array purchase plus a recurring subscription Controller refresh tied to three-year renewals, subject to program terms1516 Non-disruptive upgrades; no re-buying capacity already owned
    Evergreen//Flex Customer Capacity actually used, with fleet-level pooling Same as //Forever; capacity can be moved across the fleet Pay-per-use on owned arrays15
    Evergreen//One Everpure A performance tier, priced per unit of used capacity, with a minimum commit Everpure's responsibility, invisible to the customer Up to ten SLAs, including 99.9999% availability, a 25% capacity buffer, energy efficiency, zero planned downtime and zero data loss1718

    Table 28.1 — The three Evergreen tiers move risk progressively from the customer (owns and plans) to Everpure (owns, plans and guarantees).

    The enabler is architectural. In a Purity dual-controller array, persistent state (data, metadata, write log) lives on flash modules and NVRAM in the chassis, not in the controllers (Chapter 14), so controllers swap one at a time while the partner serves I/O, and media is replaced by adding modules and evacuating old ones.

    flowchart LR
    S["Subscription signed: Forever, Flex or One"] --> T["Pure1 telemetry: capacity, load, health"]
    T --> F["Forecast: fill date, performance headroom"]
    F --> D{"Trigger"}
    D -->|"renewal or SLA breach risk"| C["Ship new controllers"]
    D -->|"capacity or density need"| M["Ship new DirectFlash Modules"]
    C --> N["Swap one controller at a time, I/O continues"]
    M --> E["Add modules, evacuate and retire old ones"]
    N --> R["Same array, same data, newer hardware"]
    E --> R
    R --> T

    Figure 28.2 — The Evergreen loop: telemetry tells Everpure when to ship hardware, and the architecture lets that hardware go in with no data migration, which is what makes the subscription renewable.

    By July 2021 Pure Storage cited more than 7,000 non-disruptive controller upgrades for over 2,700 customers since 2015 (company-stated)19. Evergreen//One's SLAs are underwritable only because Pure1 watches every array; the buffer is sized from historical growth via Pure1's workload planner17. Overdrive, announced for Q3 2026, allows bursts up to 25% above the subscribed level20 [VERIFY: Overdrive terms from secondary summaries only].

    Worked example (illustrative). A customer uses 800 TiB under Evergreen//One. With a 25% buffer, Everpure keeps at least 1,000 TiB effective on site, on hardware it owns; if data reduces worse than planned, it adds flash at its own cost. The vendor carries utilisation and data-reduction risk a capex buyer would carry, priced into the per-TiB rate and profitable only if telemetry keeps forecasts accurate. Chapter 23 covers the cash-flow side.

    Evergreen//One bookings growth is tracked in Chapter 29.

    Fusion and Pure1: the control plane as the product¶

    flowchart TB
    U["Admins, Terraform, Ansible, AI agents via MCP"] --> API["Fusion API and Pure1 Manage MCP"]
    API --> PS["Presets: QoS, protection, tags, compliance"]
    PS --> PL["Placement engine picks array with headroom"]
    PL --> A1["FlashArray fleet"]
    PL --> A2["FlashBlade fleet"]
    PL --> A3["Everpure Cloud Dedicated"]
    A1 --> P1["Pure1 telemetry and AIOps"]
    A2 --> P1
    A3 --> P1
    P1 -->|"forecasts, drift alerts, triage"| PL

    Figure 28.3 — Users and agents ask for an outcome through one API; presets encode policy, Pure1 telemetry informs placement, and the arrays become interchangeable capacity behind it.

    Fusion has been embedded in Purity since 2024, with nothing separate to deploy21. Its central object is the preset, a fleet-wide template of performance limits, snapshot and replication policy, and tags22, much like a Kubernetes storage class. Pure1 supplies forecasts, workload planning and predictive support. In 2026 both became agent-addressable: Everpure offers a Pure1 Manage MCP server and a Fusion MCP server, so AI tools can query fleet state and carry out supervised actions23. Fusion Compliance and Agentic Triage (configuration-drift detection with AI root-cause suggestions) and Workload Rebalance and Mobility were slated for Q4 202624 [VERIFY: GA status].

    Watch adoption: over 1,200 Fusion customers against roughly 14,500–15,000 in total [VERIFY: two different base figures] is about 8%, and control-plane lock-in is real only for them.

    Portworx: storage for Kubernetes, then for VMs¶

    Portworx (acquired October 20204) pools whatever disks or cloud volumes sit under a Kubernetes cluster and gives containers persistent volumes, replication, snapshots, backup and DR, on any hardware or cloud, or directly on FlashArray. Everpure books Portworx term licences as product revenue and its other offerings as subscription services revenue3, so no standalone Portworx revenue figure is public.

    Portworx's second act came from Broadcom, whose post-2023 VMware licensing changes raised many renewal bills25 and pushed enterprises to evaluate KubeVirt, which runs VMs as Kubernetes objects (often via OpenShift Virtualization). Portworx Enterprise 3.6 (March 2026) added validated KubeVirt frameworks and a VM-readiness tool; customers run over 100,000 VM volumes on it (company-stated)26. Everpure's own IT moved more than 5,000 VMs to KubeVirt on Portworx (company-stated)27. An OpenShift plugin and an Edge SKU followed at Red Hat Summit 202628.

    Everpure hedges the VMware exit both ways: Portworx for Kubernetes, and the May 2025 Nutanix partnership (FlashArray as external AHV storage) for another hypervisor29. Either way the array can stay Everpure.

    Everpure Cloud¶

    Everpure Cloud Dedicated (ex-Cloud Block Store) is customer-managed Purity in the customer's AWS or Azure account, with replication and snapshot parity with on-premises arrays12. Everpure Cloud Azure Native, co-developed with Microsoft and launched September 202530, is a fully managed Azure Native ISV service, first for Azure VMware Solution31, with Azure VM support GA from June 202620 [VERIFY: Azure VM GA date]. The role is mainly defensive: a workload moving to cloud can stay under Fusion and Evergreen-style billing rather than native EBS or Azure Disk. Against NetApp's first-party ONTAP services in all three hyperscalers, Everpure's reach is narrower (Chapter 46).

    1touch and the data-intelligence thesis¶

    flowchart LR
    SM["Storage metadata: volumes, files, objects, owners, snapshots, access"] --> KG["Knowledge graph of data"]
    CC["Content classification: entities, PII, PHI, business meaning"] --> KG
    EX["Other estates: databases, SaaS, public cloud, third-party storage"] --> CC
    KG --> GV["Governance: residency, attribute-based access, lineage"]
    KG --> CY["Cyber: what was exposed, recover what matters first"]
    KG --> AI["AI readiness: Data Stream ingest, index, vectorize via MCP"]

    Figure 28.4 — The thesis: the storage layer already knows where every byte is and who touched it; add knowledge of what the bytes mean, and you can sell governance, cyber recovery and AI-ready data from the same graph.

    1touch closed on 2026-05-07, the $125.0M subject to adjustments and escrow3. As Everpure Data Intelligence (GA June 2026)10 it discovers structured and unstructured data, classifies it at entity level (flagging PII and PHI) into a semantic knowledge graph, tracks lineage, and enforces attribute-based access and residency policy32. Analysts also describe it as reaching databases, SaaS and third-party storage, not only Everpure arrays11. Native MCP support (September 2026) lets agents query it33. Data Stream, on FlashBlade with NVIDIA GPUs and NVIDIA's AI Data Platform reference design, ingests, indexes and vectorises that data for RAG and inference34 (Chapter 39, Chapter 33).

    Why a storage company? The array already holds the inventory (every volume, file, object, snapshot and access) and sits on the write path, so classification can trigger as data lands rather than by weekly crawls, as a catalog above storage must. Semiconductor bridge: on-die telemetry sees every transaction; external test equipment only samples.

    How "storage vendor → data platform" is meant to work commercially¶

    The model has three levers:

    1. Attach. Each layer sells into ~15,000 customers who already trust the vendor and run Pure1. Illustrative: if 5% of 15,000 customers attach Data Intelligence at an assumed $150k ARR, that is 750 × $150k ≈ $113M of ARR, about 5% on top of the $2.13B base. That is roughly one year's revenue to recover the $125M price.
    2. ARR quality. Software raises subscription mix, margin and net retention beyond capacity growth alone.
    3. Switching costs. They shift from the box (which Evergreen makes easy to refresh) to policy, automation and the knowledge graph, which are costly to rebuild elsewhere.

    Where it may not work. First, crowding: catalogs and lakehouse governance (Databricks Unity Catalog, Snowflake's governance features, Microsoft Purview) own "what is this data" for analytics, and data-security vendors (BigID, Varonis, Cyera) sell discovery to the CISO, a different buyer. Second, neutrality is unproven. One analyst said the case beyond Everpure hardware becomes "a bigger question mark", noting Everpure is outside the Open Semantic Interchange metadata effort11 [VERIFY: search-summary only]. Third, storage sellers are paid on arrays; software covering rival storage needs separate incentives. Futurum's open question: how quickly customers follow32.

    Conversation playbook¶

    Questions for a PM

    1. "What share of Data Intelligence deals cover data that is not on Everpure storage?" Strong: a number, named estate types (Oracle, SaaS, rival NAS), and how it integrates with an existing catalog or DSPM tool.
    2. "Is Fusion adoption counted as customers who enabled it or customers running presets in production?" Strong: separates "turned on" from "automating against it" and ties the latter to renewal and NDR.
    3. "What does Evergreen//One's 25% buffer cost us in deployed capacity, and how has NAND inflation changed that?" Strong: quantifies buffer and data-reduction risk and how pricing or Overdrive recovers it.

    Questions for an engineer

    1. "How does Data Intelligence classify on the write path without adding latency to Purity?" Strong: asynchronous, off the I/O path, triggered by snapshot or metadata events, with bounded controller resources.
    2. "What can an agent do through the Fusion MCP server without human approval?" Strong: scoped credentials, read-only default, approval gates on destructive actions, immutable snapshots as backstop.
    3. "When Portworx runs on non-Everpure disks, what do customers lose versus Portworx on FlashArray?" Strong: candid about data reduction, snapshot offload and replication efficiency.

    Don't say this:

    • "Evergreen means free hardware." The subscription funds upgrades, with eligibility terms.
    • "Everpure is now a data-management company like Databricks." It manages data infrastructure and metadata, not analytics engines.
    • "Portworx only works with Everpure arrays." It is hardware-agnostic by design.
    • "1touch is Everpure's biggest software deal." Portworx cost almost three times as much.
