    34. NVIDIA's ecosystem and certifications¶

    Depth: L2 · Reading time: ~10 min · Last verified: 2026-11-01 Prerequisites: Datacenter networking for storage · The GPU data path · Inference and KV cache

    TL;DR¶

    • NVIDIA runs the approved-vendor list for AI storage. Each of its reference designs (BasePOD, SuperPOD, NVIDIA Cloud Partner, Enterprise) names storage that passed NVIDIA's tests. A vendor missing from the list is usually not in the deal.
    • The program is a set of tiers ordered by scale. NVIDIA-Certified Storage has Foundation (up to 128 simulated GPUs), Enterprise (about 1,000) and NCP (up to 10,000) levels. SuperPOD certification adds per-GPU-generation throughput tiers. NCP certification adds cloud-grade uptime, durability and delivery-time rules.
    • Certification sets a floor. It does not rank vendors. The SuperPOD B200 "enhanced" tier works out to under 0.5 GB/s of read per GPU. About a dozen vendors hold NVIDIA storage certifications. Certification proves interoperability and minimum throughput. It says nothing about price, data reduction, operations or failure behaviour.
    • NVIDIA keeps moving into the storage box. The AI Data Platform (2025) put GPUs and NVIDIA retrieval software inside storage. BlueField-4 STX and CMX (2026) standardize the KV-cache tier on NVIDIA silicon, with 12 storage vendors as co-design partners.
    • NVIDIA invests in some of the vendors it certifies. It holds equity in VAST and WEKA, and NVIDIA's own clusters run on DDN. Everpure has every major certification for FlashBlade//S. It has none NVIDIA-granted yet for FlashBlade//EXA, and it has no reported NVIDIA equity tie.

    Why this matters for Everpure strategy¶

    Storage is a small slice of an AI cluster's bill of materials, but the GPU vendor sets the architecture, picks the reference partners and often owns the customer conversation. For Everpure (NYSE: P, formerly PSTG), certification is the gate to compete; the competition happens after it. The strategic questions: how fast each new product clears NVIDIA's gates, how much deal flow runs through server OEMs that sell their own storage, and what happens to array economics if NVIDIA's reference storage (STX) absorbs functions storage software performs today.

    The semiconductor parallel is close. Being on a foundry's IP ecosystem list (TSMC's Open Innovation Platform) or a reference board's approved vendor list does not win a socket, but being off it loses one. The platform owner also decides when the list is re-qualified: usually every product generation.

    Core concepts¶

    Five programs, one gatekeeper¶

    NVIDIA publishes five overlapping structures that matter for storage:

    1. DGX BasePOD. Enterprise clusters from two DGX nodes to dozens1; storage partners pass a validation-and-approval process. Launched with storage partners in September 20222.
    2. DGX SuperPOD. NVIDIA's turnkey large cluster, built from 32-node scalable units (SUs). Each GPU generation's reference architecture publishes a storage performance table to certify against3.
    3. NVIDIA Cloud Partner (NCP) reference architectures. GPU-cloud blueprints; storage earns "high-performance storage" (HPS) certification, and NCPs must meet NVIDIA's Requirements for AI Clouds4.
    4. NVIDIA-Certified Storage (NVCS). Launched at GTC in March 2025 for enterprise AI factories on NVIDIA-Certified servers5, with Foundation, Enterprise and NCP levels6.
    5. AI Data Platform and STX. Reference designs for what storage itself should contain, not test suites. NVCS is the prerequisite for building on the AI Data Platform5.
    flowchart LR
    G["GDS support or self-certification"] --> F["NVCS Foundation: simulates up to 128 GPUs"]
    F --> E["NVCS Enterprise: about 1,000 GPUs"]
    E --> B["DGX BasePOD validation: 2 to dozens of DGX nodes"]
    B --> S["DGX SuperPOD: per-SU read and write tiers, re-run per GPU generation"]

    Figure 34.1 — Each rung simulates or deploys more GPUs and adds tests; the programs are parallel, not formal prerequisites, but buyers read them as a ladder.

    What the tests actually require¶

    Most test details sit on partner portals. Three public anchors show the bar:

    • NVCS scale. Foundation tests simulate workloads of up to 128 GPUs, Enterprise about 1,000 and NCP up to 10,0006 [VERIFY: Foundation 128 and the 10,000-GPU top of the ladder are corroborated by NVIDIA docs summaries; the ~1,000-GPU Enterprise figure is not]. NVIDIA's framework covers training, fine-tuning, inference, KV cache and retrieval I/O profiles, including synchronous and asynchronous checkpoints6.
    • SuperPOD throughput. The DGX B200 reference architecture asks, per 256-GPU SU, 40/20 GB/s read/write at "standard" and 125/62 GB/s at "enhanced", scaling linearly to four SUs3. The B300 version asks 640 and 2,000 GB/s read for eight SUs7.
    • NCP operations. High-speed file storage at roughly 99.5% uptime and 99.999% annual durability per PB, NFSv4 home directories, and storage live with API integration eight weeks before the GPUs arrive4 [VERIFY: thresholds quoted from v2.2; current is v2.4]. The detailed GB300 NVL72 storage design is on NVIDIA's non-public partner portal8.

    Worked example: how high is the floor? Enhanced B200 read is 125 GB/s ÷ 256 GPUs ≈ 0.49 GB/s per GPU; standard is 40 ÷ 256 ≈ 0.16 GB/s. NVIDIA's own guidance says read-dominant work (image or video training) wants about 4 GB/s per GPU3, eight times the enhanced tier, or ~1 TB/s per SU. WEKA claimed 720 GB/s of read from one eight-node WEKApod at its 2024 SuperPOD certification9, beating the four-SU enhanced target with a single small appliance. Certification says the system won't starve a text-LLM training job; it does not separate the top vendors.

    What certification does and doesn't prove

    Certification shows Certification does not show
    Interoperates with NVIDIA's stack (GDS, RDMA, Spectrum-X or InfiniBand, Base Command) Price-performance, $/TB or power per TB
    Meets a published throughput floor at a stated scale Behaviour at 80% full, during rebuilds, or under mixed tenants
    NVIDIA support and sales teams know the configuration Effective capacity after data reduction
    The vendor will re-qualify on the next GPU generation Long-run reliability, upgrade disruption, operations staffing
    Inclusion in reference bills of materials Any ranking: the list is unordered and non-exclusive

    Table 34.1 — Certification is a gate, not a grade. Everything in the right column is where deals are actually won (see Chapter 37 on reading benchmarks).

    The AI Data Platform: NVIDIA's blueprint for the storage box itself¶

    In March 2025 NVIDIA presented the AI Data Platform, a reference design that puts accelerated compute and AI agents into the storage layer so data is extracted, embedded and indexed where it lives rather than copied to a separate RAG pipeline. NVIDIA named ten launch partners, Pure Storage among them10. The hardware recipe pairs RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs with BlueField DPUs and Spectrum-X Ethernet. The software recipe is the AI-Q blueprint, which calls NeMo Retriever microservices for extraction and retrieval11. Later implementations go further: Hammerspace's platform (GA March 2026) bundles NIM and NeMo Retriever12, and Everpure's Data Stream (June 2026) is an ingest-to-inference pipeline on the same design13.

    The bargain cuts both ways: partners get a ready-made RAG answer (Chapter 33), NVIDIA gets GPUs and software subscriptions in every storage rack, and every partner's AI story converges on the same NVIDIA parts.

    STX and CMX: the reference storage server¶

    NVIDIA went a step further at CES in January 2026 with the Inference Context Memory Storage (ICMS) platform, a BlueField-4-powered KV-cache tier inside each pod14. It sits between Dynamo's local-SSD (G3) and networked-storage (G4) tiers, hence the "G3.5" label in Chapter 32. At GTC in March 2026 it widened this into BlueField-4 STX, a modular storage reference architecture built on Vera Rubin, BlueField-4 and Spectrum-X. The first rack-scale implementation is the CMX context memory storage platform, effectively the CES platform under a new name15. Twelve storage vendors, including Everpure, VAST, WEKA, DDN, Dell and NetApp, are co-design partners; AIC, Supermicro and QCT build hardware; CoreWeave, Lambda, Nebius, OCI and four others are early adopters16. STX is a blueprint NVIDIA distributes, not a box it sells17. KV-cache mechanics are in Chapter 32, and the DPU's place in the network is in Chapter 10.

    The strategic point is where value moves. If the controller is a BlueField-4 running NVIDIA's DOCA software on ODM-built servers, storage software becomes a tenant on NVIDIA's controller silicon, as fabless designs build on a foundry's process and IP. One analyst firm argues vendors not optimized for inference and context memory face margin pressure as NVIDIA standardizes the data path, while noting those vendors are STX partners18. Opinion, but the right risk.

    The paths have different economics. Dell bundles PowerScale, ObjectScale and its Lightning parallel file system into its AI Data Platform with NVIDIA19, so an independent vendor in a Dell-led deal starts behind. HPE sells its own X10000 alongside GreenLake for File, which runs VAST software20. Cisco has no storage, so its Secure AI Factory with NVIDIA lists partners (Pure Storage, Hitachi Vantara, NetApp, VAST, later Nutanix)21, which is why Everpure's FlashStack CVD in the Cisco AI PODs matters22. Neoclouds qualify several vendors: CoreWeave lists VAST, WEKA, DDN, IBM and Pure Storage23, and its US$1.17 billion VAST agreement shows how large one decision can be24. See Chapter 35.

    Kingmaker or toll booth? NVIDIA's investments¶

    NVIDIA also invests in some listed vendors. It participated in VAST's 2021 Series D25 and its 2026 Series F at a US$30 billion valuation (company-stated)26. Secondary sources also list NVIDIA in VAST's December 2023 Series E, but VAST's own release names other investors [VERIFY: NVIDIA in VAST Series E]. NVIDIA was in WEKA's US$140 million Series E in 2024 (company-stated)27. DDN has no reported NVIDIA equity; its tie is operational, running NVIDIA's Selene and Eos clusters (Eos: 48 AI400X2 units, ~4 TB/s, company-stated)28. No public evidence shows certifications favour NVIDIA's portfolio companies; the pass lists include almost everyone. The investments buy something less formal: early roadmap access, joint launches (VAST's CNode-X, February 2026) and the "NVIDIA-backed" label in every press article.

    Read honestly, NVIDIA is less a kingmaker than a toll booth with favoured lanes. It rarely picks one winner; it sets the entry fee (re-certify every GPU generation, adopt each new NVIDIA interface) and profits from a crowded field of compliant suppliers. That is a platform owner commoditizing its complements.

    Everpure's position. FlashBlade//S holds the full set. The gap is FlashBlade//EXA, aimed at the largest AI and neocloud deals. In March 2026 Everpure said it was extending NVCS validation to EXA as a step toward the NCP level, and aligning EXA with STX31. Trade press reported NCP certification for "later this year", but no completed EXA certification had been published by 2026-11-01. Until one is, competitors can call EXA uncertified in exactly the deals it targets. Everpure's distinctive move is public, peer-reviewed benchmarking: it submitted to MLPerf Storage v3.0 in September 2026, while VAST, WEKA and DDN did not45 (DDN and WEKA entered earlier rounds; VAST never has44). The results are vendor-run, so they show what each submitter's configuration can do, not a market ranking.

    Steelmanning the others. DDN's claim is strongest: NVIDIA runs its own clusters on DDN, which no certificate matches. VAST and WEKA have equity ties and usually ship NVIDIA's new interfaces first. Dell and HPE own the server sale. Everpure's counter is what certification doesn't measure: Evergreen economics, power and density (Chapter 38) and enterprise data services.

    Options a strategy team might evaluate, based on public information:

    1. Certification velocity as a KPI. Treat "days from NVIDIA GPU launch to certified on each tier" as a product metric, with EXA at the NCP level as the near-term gap.
    2. Be an early STX/CMX implementer so Purity's data services run on whatever controller NVIDIA standardizes, rather than defending x86 controllers.
    3. Diversify routes to market beyond NVIDIA-led deals: Cisco, integrators, hyperscaler marketplaces, and non-NVIDIA accelerator and Ultra Ethernet ecosystems as hedges.
    4. Compete on what certificates don't measure: independent benchmarks, effective $/TB, TB per watt and non-disruptive upgrades.
    5. Weigh a deeper NVIDIA commercial or equity tie against the cost: dependence on one supplier's roadmap and less neutrality with AMD and hyperscaler buyers.

    Conversation playbook¶

    Questions for a PM

    1. "When will FlashBlade//EXA appear on NVIDIA's certified list, at which level, and which deals are we losing until then?" Strong: a level, a GPU generation, a target quarter, and the deal types where the gap is used against EXA.
    2. "In a Dell- or HPE-led AI factory, how do we get in?" Strong: separates customer-mandated storage from OEM default, cites Cisco and integrator routes, and sizes each path.
    3. "What does STX/CMX do to our bill of materials and gross margin?" Strong: whether Purity runs on BlueField-4, who builds the hardware, and whether CMX is new revenue or cannibalizes array capacity.

    Questions for an engineer

    1. "How much headroom do we have over the SuperPOD enhanced tier per SU, and what limits us first?" Strong: a multiple (say 3× or 5×) and the first bottleneck (front-end ports, metadata, back-end fabric).
    2. "What did certification testing not exercise that customers hit in production?" Strong: rebuild under load, full-system behaviour, multi-tenant QoS, upgrade under load.
    3. "What would it take to run our data path on a BlueField-4 controller?" Strong: core-count and memory constraints, which Purity services fit on the DPU, and what stays on x86.

    Don't say this:

    • "We're NVIDIA-certified, so we're as good as VAST." Everyone is; it is the entry ticket.
    • "SuperPOD certification proves top performance." It proves a floor of about 0.5 GB/s per GPU on B200 enhanced.
    • "NVIDIA picks the storage winner." NVIDIA usually lists a dozen. OEMs, integrators and neocloud bake-offs decide most deals.
    • "STX means NVIDIA is entering storage." It is a partner-built reference architecture; the risk is a commoditized controller, not an NVIDIA storage brand (yet).

    Key terms¶

    • DGX BasePOD / SuperPOD — enterprise and large-cluster reference architectures.
    • Scalable unit (SU) — the 32-node building block of a SuperPOD.
    • NCP / HPS — NVIDIA Cloud Partner program, and its high-performance storage certification.
    • NVIDIA-Certified Storage — tiered storage certification (Foundation, Enterprise, NCP).
    • AI Data Platform — NVIDIA's reference design for GPU-accelerated retrieval inside storage.
    • BlueField-4 STX / CMX — NVIDIA's storage reference architecture, and its context-memory rack implementation.
    • AI-Q, NeMo Retriever, NIM — agent blueprint, retrieval and inference microservices.
