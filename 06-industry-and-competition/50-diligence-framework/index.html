    50. Technical diligence framework for storage/AI-data targets¶

    Depth: L2 · Reading time: ~10 min · Last verified: 2026-11-01 Prerequisites: Architecture decision matrix · Reading benchmarks · Distributed systems fundamentals · WEKA deep dive

    TL;DR¶

    • Storage diligence asks one question: will this code keep other people's data safe, fast and supportable at ten times today's scale? The process is standard software M&A. The critical risks (durability bugs, metadata walls, support load) do not appear in a data room by default.
    • Treat every performance number as a claim until you reproduce it. Normalise it per node, per rack unit, per kW and per usable TB. Then run your own proof of concept (PoC) on your own workload shape (Chapter 37).
    • Field data beats models. Annualised failure rates, rebuild-time distributions, data-loss incident logs and phone-home telemetry tell you more than any "eleven nines" durability figure.
    • Integration fit with Everpure has four seams: control plane, data path, licensing and channel. The data path is the expensive one. Purity and DirectFlash assume a single operating system managing raw NAND, so a second file system is a second product line, not a feature.
    • Public data can score a target, but only roughly. In the WEKA worked example, 38% of the weight rests on low-confidence scores, four of them placeholders; the score is 2.8 of 5, with a plausible range of 2.1 to 3.7.

    Why this matters for Everpure strategy¶

    The AI storage cycle has produced private companies with large valuations and immature evidence. WEKA was last priced at US$1.6B in May 20241 and VAST at a reported US$30B in April 20262. Any build/buy/partner discussion at Everpure (NYSE: P, formerly PSTG) about AI data infrastructure (Chapter 54) ends in questions a financial model cannot answer. Does it scale past the customer's next cluster? Has it ever lost data? Could it run under Fusion and on DirectFlash? Everpure's record is small and mid-sized software deals, such as Portworx at US$352.9M plus US$32.2M of retention payments3 and 1touch at US$125.0M in 20264. A frontier-AI target would be a different order of risk.

    An ex-semis dealmaker already knows the analogous discipline. You would not buy a chip company on its datasheet. You would ask for characterisation across process corners, the errata list, RMA history, the tape-out record and third-party IP licences. Storage diligence maps onto each. Benchmarks are the datasheet. Data-loss incidents are the errata, except that they destroy customer data. Field failure rates are the RMA history, and open-source dependencies are the third-party IP.

    Figure 50.1 — Technical diligence is a funnel with three kill gates; the cheap public teardown should kill most targets before anyone signs an NDA.

    Access gets more expensive at each stage. The public-info teardown reads documentation, patents, benchmark submissions, GitHub repositories, job postings and certification lists; vendor documentation is far more candid about limits than marketing. Management technical sessions under NDA should be run by your own architects, not the corp-dev team. The PoC is where most deals should die if they are going to: the acquirer's workload shape, ideally its hardware, and its engineers at the keyboard. Reference calls should include a churned customer and the largest deployment, not just the three the target offers. Code review comes last, under a clean-team protocol, because it exposes trade secrets and, if the deal fails, contaminates the acquirer's engineers.

    The dimensions that matter¶

    Architecture and its walls. Every architecture class has a known first wall (Chapter 24). Shared-nothing clusters hit rebalancing and east-west traffic (Chapter 15). Disaggregated shared-everything designs hit fabric and enclosure failure domains (Chapter 16). Parallel file systems hit the metadata server (Chapter 17). Good teams name their own wall in the first session.

    Performance claims. Normalise before comparing. Suppose a vendor claims 1 TB/s from 40 nodes. That is 25 GB/s per node. If each node has 2×400 GbE (100 GB/s of line rate), the system runs at 25% of wire speed. That is either honest headroom or a software ceiling, and only a PoC tells you which. Chapter 37 lists the gaming techniques.

    Scalability and metadata. Capacity, performance and metadata are three separate curves, and vendors usually quote only the flattering one (Chapter 21). The semiconductor bridge is tag RAM. A centralised metadata server is a single tag array that every access must consult, so it saturates. Sharded metadata spreads the load, but operations spanning shards, such as renames and snapshots, become distributed transactions.

    Durability and data integrity. This is the dimension most likely to destroy deal value, and the one data rooms least often cover. Durability in "nines" is a model output, a mean time to data loss (MTTDL) estimate (Chapter 20), not a field fact. Field evidence is what counts. As a public baseline, Backblaze reported a 1.36% annualised failure rate (AFR) across about 344,000 hard drives in 20255. Worked example: a target with 500 clusters averaging 200 drives has 100,000 drives in the field. At 1% AFR that is 1,000 failures a year, about three rebuilds a day somewhere in the fleet. The question is how many rebuilds overlapped a second fault, and what happened then. Telemetry is also how the industry measured silent corruption: a FAST '08 study used NetApp call-home data from 1.53 million drives over 41 months6. A target without equivalent telemetry cannot answer at all.

    Security and roadmap. Ransomware resilience means snapshots that an administrator credential cannot delete (Everpure's equivalent is SafeMode) and multi-party approval for destructive operations. Treat the roadmap as a tape-out record: dated announcements against dated general availability (GA). Slips are normal; re-announcements are not.

    Code base and team. Map who wrote the data path and metadata engine, and whether they will stay. Run software composition analysis for open-source licences, because copyleft travels. Lustre is GPLv27, so distributed modifications to its kernel modules carry source obligations. MinIO moved its server to AGPLv3 in 20218, whose obligations can be triggered by network use. Disputes happen. In March 2023 MinIO said it had revoked WEKA's licences, and WEKA replied that its fork derived only from Apache 2.0 code9. No public resolution was found.

    Support burden. Supporting many servers, NICs, kernels and clouds means a large qualification matrix, a cost hidden in operating expenses rather than gross margin. Telemetry is itself an asset. HPE bought Nimble in 2017 largely for InfoSight10, and Pure1 META has run on phone-home data since 201711.

    Hardware and supply chain. An appliance vendor carries NAND exposure directly; Everpure raised prices in February 2026 as NAND costs surged12 (Chapter 48). A software-only vendor pushes that exposure to customers but depends on partners' goodwill.

    Customer concentration. AI-native storage revenue clusters in a few neoclouds and model builders whose spend follows GPU deliveries (Chapter 35).

    Unit economics. Gross margin depends on what is sold. Illustratively, software at 85% gross margin and pass-through hardware at 20% blend to 0.6 × 85% + 0.4 × 20% = 59% when hardware is 40% of revenue. Margin can move with packaging mix, independent of pricing power. For reference, Everpure's non-GAAP Q2 FY27 gross margin was 66.2% on product and 74.9% on subscription13 (Chapter 29).

    Figure 50.2 — Control-plane federation takes months and data-path convergence years (illustrative), because Purity manages raw NAND that the target's software has never seen.

    Control plane. Fusion is the fleet control plane at the core of the Enterprise Data Cloud14 (Chapter 22); exposing a target's clusters to it through APIs is the cheapest integration. Data path. Purity runs the flash translation layer (FTL) across DirectFlash modules itself (Chapter 26). A target built for commodity SSDs runs as a parallel stack or is ported, a multi-year job. Licensing. Evergreen//One SLAs commit Everpure to performance and durability guarantees (Chapter 28); anything sold under them inherits that bar. Channel. A target's OEM partners may be Everpure's competitors.

    Worked example: WEKA, public data only¶

This is a method illustration, not a recommendation, and it implies nothing about non-public Everpure plans. Where public data is silent, the dimension gets a placeholder 3 with low confidence. Customer concentration is the one low-confidence dimension scored below 3, because the visible references lean on a few neoclouds and labs.

    Reading the result. The weighted score is 283 ÷ 100 = 2.83. The five low-confidence dimensions (scalability, durability, security, customers, economics) carry 38 points of weight. Scoring them all 1 gives 2.14, and scoring them all 5 gives 3.66. Public data cannot tell a mediocre target from a good one, but it does set the agenda: field durability data, customer concentration and margin mix come first.

    Options a strategy team might evaluate, based on public information. The score favours lighter-touch options over a full acquisition. These include interoperability (Everpure object storage as a WEKA tier), competing head-on with PureKVA31 against AMG, or monitoring signals such as financing events and MLPerf participation (Chapter 43). The integration score of 2 reflects data-path overlap, the costliest seam. The same template applied to VAST (Chapter 42) or DDN (Chapter 44) would raise other questions: valuation and data-reduction IP for VAST, Lustre GPL obligations and appliance margin for DDN.

    Table 50.4 — Storage acquirers have mostly kept acquired data paths as separate product lines rather than merging them, which is the pattern a diligence plan should price in.

    Dell and HPE run several data paths side by side. Everpure's moat is one OS (Purity) on one hardware architecture (DirectFlash) under one subscription model (Evergreen), so the data-path seam costs Everpure more than it costs Dell: a second file system weakens the one-platform story. The steelman for the portfolio approach is speed, since a portfolio vendor owns a segment the day a deal closes. Everpure's approach, building EXA in-house and buying software around the edges (Portworx, 1touch), keeps the platform coherent at the cost of time to market.

    History and trajectory¶

    The focus of storage diligence has shifted with each wave. When EMC bought XtremIO before it had product revenue36, the bet was almost entirely architecture and team. HPE's Nimble deal made installed-base telemetry an asset in its own right10. Portworx moved the focus to cloud-native software and engineer retention3. AI targets add three layers: GPU-path claims such as KV-cache offload, which are rarely audited; NVIDIA certification status, which partners do not control (Chapter 34); and neocloud concentration. Valuations have outrun evidence. Audited KV-cache benchmarks began only with MLPerf Storage v3.0 in September 202618, so expect the PoC and reference-call stages to carry more weight through 2027.

    Conversation playbook¶

    Questions for a PM

    1. "If we sold this under Evergreen//One, which SLAs could we sign on day one?" A strong answer names the metrics (performance per TB, durability, uptime), the telemetry needed to measure them, and the gap.
    2. "Which of the target's top customers are in our pipeline, and who resells it today?" A strong answer identifies overlap and flags OEM partners that compete with Everpure.
    3. "What does the target ship that our roadmap won't have within 18 months?" A strong answer names one dated capability and compares build time against deal time.

    Questions for an engineer

    1. "Where does this architecture hit its first wall, and how close is the largest customer?" A strong answer names the wall by architecture class and gives the largest deployment's metadata operation rate, not only its capacity.
    2. "Walk me through the worst data-integrity bug they shipped." A strong answer covers how it was found, how many systems it affected, and whether a fleet-wide scan confirmed the fix.
    3. "What would it take to run their data path on DirectFlash and under Fusion?" A strong answer separates API federation (months) from FTL assumptions (years) and names the modules to rewrite.

    Don't say this:

    • "Their benchmark proves it scales." One hero configuration proves only that configuration.
    • "Software-only means no hardware risk." The risk moves into the qualification matrix, partner defection and support cost.
    • "Eleven nines is their field record." Nines come from an MTTDL model; field data is incident logs.
    • "Open source means free." GPL and AGPL obligations are diligence items, and licence disputes happen.
    • "We'll plug it into Fusion." That covers the control plane only, the cheapest of the four seams.

    Key terms¶

    • Technical due diligence — verifying a target's technology claims, risks and integration cost before a deal (see the glossary).
    • Proof of concept (PoC) — a hands-on test of the target's system on the buyer's workloads.
    • Clean team — a ring-fenced group allowed to see competitively sensitive material such as source code.
    • Software composition analysis — automated scanning of code for open-source components and their licences.
    • Copyleft — licence terms (GPL, AGPL) requiring derived works to be shared under the same terms.
    • Data-loss incident log — a vendor's record of every case of lost or corrupted customer data.
    • Key-person risk — dependence on a few engineers whose departure would stall the product.
    • Integration seam — one of the four places a target must join Everpure: control plane, data path, licensing, channel.
    • Placeholder score — a neutral score assigned where public data is silent, flagged for diligence.
