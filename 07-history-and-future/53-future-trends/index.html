    53. Future trends, 2026–2032¶

    Depth: L2 · Reading time: ~14 min · Last verified: 2026-11-01 Prerequisites: Media physics · Interfaces and emerging tech · Inference and KV cache · Power and density · History of flash

    TL;DR¶

    • Capacity per device is the most certain trend, and Everpure's main lever. DirectFlash Modules (DFMs) are at 300 TB, standard SSDs at 245.76 TB, and 512 TB drives are on 2027 roadmaps. The scarce resources are rebuild time, NAND supply and watts, not slots.
    • Disk is not dying this decade. Vendor HAMR roadmaps take nearline drives from 44 TB toward 100 TB by about 2029, and Seagate says its output is largely sold out through 2028. Flash takes performance and power-constrained AI capacity; disk keeps cold bulk.
    • AI inference creates a new tier between HBM and shared storage. NVIDIA's KV cache hierarchy (Dynamo KVBM, BlueField-4 STX/CMX) makes context memory a purchasable category from 2H 2026. Every storage vendor, Everpure included, is fighting to be its backend.
    • The value is moving up into data services and down into the fabric. Catalogs, governance and vector search are converging with storage. DPUs, Ultra Ethernet and S3 over RDMA are commoditizing the data path.
    • Be calibrated. CXL pooling, co-packaged optics in storage and PLC flash are real but later and smaller than vendor decks imply. DNA and glass archives are pre-commercial. Several of today's "certainties" are vendor roadmaps that have slipped before.

    Why this matters for Everpure strategy¶

    Everpure (NYSE: P, formerly PSTG) has built its strategy on three bets that run through this window: flash replaces disk at hyperscale, its own flash modules stay ahead of commodity SSDs, and it can move up from storage into data management (the 1touch acquisition, Data Intelligence, the Everpure rename itself). Each bet depends on the trends below. A NAND cost curve that stalls, a HAMR ramp that holds, or a commodity 512 TB SSD that arrives on time each change the economics of the hyperscaler licensing model. KV cache tiers and sovereign AI decide which new buyers exist.

    This chapter gives each of twelve trends a likelihood, a timing call, winners and losers, and options a strategy team might evaluate, based on public information. It separates vendor roadmaps (what a supplier says it will ship) from independent forecasts (analysts, standards bodies and observed adoption). The strategic responses are developed in Chapter 54.

    Core concepts¶

    How to read the calls¶

    Each trend gets three calls. Likelihood is the probability that the trend is material by 2032, meaning it changes a meaningful share of storage buying decisions. High means 70% or more, Medium 40–70%, Low under 40%. Timing is when it becomes material, which is usually two to four years after the first product ships. Evidence is labelled as vendor roadmap or independent signal. Vendor roadmaps slip, but unevenly. Seagate's 50 TB HAMR drive was once planned for 2026 and now enters qualification in late 20271, and the LTO tape consortium cut its future capacities in 20252. Roadmaps a vendor controls more fully can fare better: Pure Storage set a 300 TB DFM target for 2026 in March 20233 and met it, though that is one data point, not a rule.

    Media¶

    1. Larger flash modules: 300 TB, then 600 TB, then 1 PB. Everpure's DFMs are at 300 TB per module4. The largest standard SSD, Micron's 6600 ION, shipped at 245.76 TB in May 20265. A co-founder has described 600 TB and then 1 PB-plus modules, without dates6 [VERIFY: search-summary only]. The commodity side is closing in: Sandisk's roadmap shows 512 TB in 20277, and Meta has said it plans QLC drives up to 512 TB8 [VERIFY: source reused from Ch 26; confirm Meta statement]. Vendor roadmap on both sides. The independent signal is TrendForce's report that hyperscalers are buying QLC for AI workloads in 4Q269.

    The constraint shifts from slots to failure physics. Take 24 modules per enclosure. At 300 TB that is 7.2 PB raw; at 1 PB it is 24 PB. Rebuilding one failed module at 1 GB/s takes 300,000 s (about 83 hours) at 300 TB and 1,000,000 s (about 11.6 days) at 1 PB. Spread across many modules at an aggregate 20 GB/s, the same rebuilds take about 4.2 and 13.9 hours. So bigger modules reward vendors who own the FTL and the rebuild path, which is the DirectFlash argument (Chapter 26). Call: High likelihood; 600 TB-class in 2027–28; 1 PB-class by 2029–30. The timing is an extrapolation of the 2023–26 doubling pace (75 → 150 → 300 TB), not a dated Everpure roadmap; none has been published for 600 TB or 1 PB.

    2. PLC and 400–1,000-layer NAND. Samsung's V10 passes 400 layers using hybrid bonding10, while Kioxia/Sandisk's 332-layer BiCS10 claims higher density per mm²11. Bit density, not layer count, is the metric. Kioxia has described a technical path to 1,000 layers by 202712, and Samsung was reported to target 1,000 layers by 203013. Vendor roadmaps, and they conflict. PLC (5 bits per cell) adds only 25% more bits than QLC, against 33% for TLC to QLC, and halves the voltage margin again (32 states versus 16). SK hynix's split-cell PLC is research presented at IEDM in December 202514. No product is announced. Call: layer scaling High and continuous; PLC Medium at best, 2029–32, and probably first in read-mostly archive flash. The semis frame: layer count is the new node name. It is a marketing number that diverges from cost per bit once string stacking and bonding add process steps.

    3. HDD's future: HAMR, nearline and archive. Seagate says its Mozaic 5 platform (more than 5 TB per platter, so 50 TB-plus drives) enters customer qualification in late 2027, with nearline output largely committed through 20281. Western Digital targets HAMR ramping in 2027, 60 TB ePMR and 100 TB HAMR by 202915. Vendor roadmaps, but the sold-out order books are an independent-ish demand signal. In 2026 enterprise SSDs cost roughly 18.6× HDD per TB16, far above the ~10× of 2020. The weakness is access density. A 50 TB drive at roughly 250–300 MB/s takes about 50 hours to read end to end, so IOPS and bandwidth per TB fall with every generation. Call: High that disk keeps the nearline bulk tier through 2032; flash takes the performance and power-constrained share. The winners are the two HAMR suppliers and the object and tiering vendors that blend disk with flash. The loser is any flash vendor whose case rests on purchase price rather than watts, density and operations (Chapter 38).

    11. DNA, glass and ceramic archival. Microsoft says Project Silica's research phase finished in 2025, with lifetime claims beyond 10,000 years, but has announced no product17. Cerabyte targets 100 PB-plus ceramic archive racks by 2030. Its strategic investors include Western Digital (2025) and Pure Storage itself (July 2024, with co-founder John Colgrove joining Cerabyte's board), so Everpure is not a neutral observer of this trend18. Atlas Data Storage spun out of Twist Bioscience in May 2025 with US$155 million to commercialize DNA storage19. Write throughput is the gating problem: DNA synthesis needs orders-of-magnitude gains. Call: Low likelihood of material impact by 2032. Watch for hyperscaler production use, not lab capacity records.

    Interconnect and architecture¶

    4. CXL memory pooling and tiers. CXL 4.0 (November 2025) runs at 128 GT/s on the PCIe 7.0 PHY20, and PCIe 8.0 targets a 2028 final spec21. Direct-attached CXL memory expanders ship, while switched pooling is only starting (Chapter 12). The independent evidence is sobering. Microsoft's Pond study found pooling across 16 sockets cut DRAM needs by about 7%22. Forecasts for the CXL market in 2028 range from about US$3.5 billion to more than US$15 billion23. The new use case is KV cache: Samsung and AMD showed CXL pooling cutting time to first token by 38% at OCP 2026 (vendor demo)24. CXL memory is still DRAM priced like DRAM, so it is a faster tier, not a cheaper one. Call: Medium; 2029–32 for storage relevance; it competes with flash for KV cache only at the hot edge.

    5. Disaggregation everywhere, including DPUs. Disaggregated designs are now mainstream. VAST built the category, NetApp's AFX scales controllers and capacity independently25, and Everpure's FlashBlade//EXA splits metadata from data (Chapter 16). The next step moves storage software onto DPUs. NVIDIA's BlueField-4 STX reference architecture launched in March 2026 with twelve storage co-design partners, Everpure among them2627. VAST runs its controller software on BlueField-4 inside GPU servers, with hardware due in 2H 202628. The semis analogy is the chiplet I/O die. Control moves into a standard part whose roadmap the platform owner, NVIDIA, sets. Call: High; adopt now. Disaggregation itself becomes table stakes. The open question is who owns the DPU software slot.

    6. Photonic interconnects and co-packaged optics (CPO). CPO puts the optical engine next to the switch ASIC, cutting pluggable-module power and lasers. NVIDIA said in May 2026 that Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics switches were in production, with "4x fewer lasers" (vendor claim)29. TrendForce reported shipments to some partners by July 2026; broad availability is not yet confirmed. Its Kyber racks for Rubin Ultra (2027) will scale up over copper and CPO30. Broadcom ships 102.4 Tb/s Tomahawk 6 silicon31. Vendor roadmaps throughout. Storage rides on the same switches but rarely drives them, and storage NICs and arrays stay pluggable for serviceability. Call: Medium; CPO in AI scale-out switches by 2027–28; storage-side optics changes 2029–32. Few direct consequences for array vendors beyond rack power budgets.

    7. KV cache as a new tier ("context memory"). Inference keeps each session's attention keys and values. A Llama 3.1 70B-class model needs 320 KiB per token, so a 128K-token session is about 43 GB (Chapter 32). Keeping 10,000 such sessions warm takes about 430 TB, or one to two 300 TB modules. NVIDIA's Dynamo KV Block Manager defines tiers G1 (HBM) to G4 (networked storage)32, and BlueField-4 context memory storage (CMX) adds a pod-level tier, with availability in 2H 202633. The independent signals are MLPerf Storage v3.0 adding a KV cache workload in September 202634 and TrendForce linking QLC demand to KV cache9. Everpure (PureKVA)35, WEKA (Augmented Memory Grid)36, DDN, VAST and HPE all ship offerings. The semis bridge is a victim cache. Blocks evicted from HBM drop into host DRAM, then into pod flash, then into shared storage, and a hit at any level saves recomputing the prefill. As with an L3 cache, value depends on hit rate and fill bandwidth, not capacity alone. Call: High; material in 2027–28. The risk is that NVIDIA's reference design turns the tier into a commodity flash box.

    Data services¶

    8. Data-management convergence: storage, catalog, governance and vector. Storage vendors are absorbing the layers above them. Everpure bought 1touch for US$125.0 million37 and shipped Data Stream on NVIDIA's AI Data Platform design38. VAST sells a database and agent engine, and AWS put vector indexes (S3 Vectors, generally available December 2025) inside object storage39. The nearest independent forecast is narrower. Gartner predicts that by 2029 more than 80% of on-premises unstructured data will sit on consolidated file-and-object platforms, up from 50% in 202540. Its 2023 version predicted 70% by 202841, so the forecast has been raised, not delayed. But it measures file-and-object consolidation, not storage vendors absorbing catalogs and governance, so it supports the platform half of this trend only. The steelman against: Databricks, Snowflake and the security vendors own the buyer and see every vendor's storage (Chapter 39). Call: High that storage vendors ship these features; Medium that they capture the economics; 2027–28.

    10. Object as the primary AI interface; S3 over RDMA. S3 over RDMA keeps HTTP for authentication and moves bulk bytes over RDMA into GPU memory. MinIO shipped it in November 202442, NVIDIA productized the pattern for Cloudian, Dell and HPE in 202543, and Pure Storage announced it for FlashBlade44. About one-sixth of MLPerf Storage v3.0 submissions used S334 (Chapter 9). A 400 GbE port moves 50 GB/s, which classic S3 over TCP and TLS cannot fill per client without heavy CPU. RDMA removes the reason to buy a parallel file system for many AI reads. Call: High; 2027–28. It erodes the file-system moat of AI-native vendors and commoditizes object.

    Deployment and operating models¶

    9. Sovereign and edge AI storage. Gartner forecasts sovereign cloud IaaS spending of about US$80 billion in 2026, up 35.6%, with Europe overtaking North America in 202745. IDC reports the fastest AI infrastructure growth in Saudi Arabia and the UAE, driven by sovereign programs46. These are independent forecasts. DDN launched sovereign AI blueprints at SC2547, and Portworx added an edge SKU in 202648. Sovereign buyers want on-premises control, local support, air-gap options and no dependence on a US hyperscaler control plane. That favors appliance vendors and open, portable software. Call: High; adopt now. Volumes are lumpy national deals; edge AI storage stays small per site.

    12. AI-driven operations. Storage AIOps is two decades old. What is new is agents that act. Everpure made Pure1 "agentic" through read-only MCP servers, with Copilot moving toward guided triage49. Gartner reportedly predicts that fewer than 1% of storage leaders run agentic storage management today, rising to 20% by 202850 [VERIFY: search-summary only]. Trust, not capability, is the bottleneck: agents that change production state need audit, rollback and blast-radius limits. Call: Medium; assistive agents now, write-capable autonomy for routine tasks in 2027–28.

    The steelman for competitors cuts several ways. VAST is most aligned with disaggregation, DPUs and convergence. It already runs on BlueField-4 and sells a database layer, so trends 5, 7 and 8 play to its strengths. WEKA and DDN hold the strongest KV cache and HPC positions. Dell, NetApp and HPE have the reach for sovereign deals and full-stack bundles. Hyperscalers pull convergence into S3 itself. Everpure's distinct asset is trend 1. No competitor ships a proprietary flash module at 300 TB (IBM's FlashCore Module is the nearest analogue), and the hyperscaler design wins monetize it without NAND exposure51. The steelman against: commodity 512 TB SSDs and FDP could narrow that lead within one product cycle. Its main exposure is that NVIDIA's STX turns trends 5 and 7 into reference designs where storage software is a feature on someone else's hardware.

    The options sort into two groups. No-regret moves pay off under almost any scenario: dated module roadmaps, default integration with Dynamo and NIXL, RDMA-capable S3 everywhere, and publishing cost-per-token rather than TTFT multipliers. Real options are cheap to hold now and valuable only if a trend breaks favorably: early PLC qualification for archive flash, running Purity services on DPUs, and sovereign reference designs with local partners. CXL, co-packaged optics and DNA or glass archives need monitoring, not investment. A strategy team might rank build, buy or partner choices for each group in Chapter 54.

    History and trajectory¶

    Storage forecasts have a poor record, and in a consistent direction. Every new tier was predicted to arrive faster than it did. Intel's Optane was meant to be the persistent-memory tier; Intel wound it down in 2022 with a US$559 million charge52. ZNS SSDs were meant to remake flash management; the gentler Flexible Data Placement (FDP), driven by Meta and Google, has drawn more hyperscaler support53. "Flash kills disk by 2026" missed because NAND prices rose: enterprise SSD premiums over disk widened in 2026 instead of narrowing16. Forecasts based on supply-side physics (density, layers, PCIe doubling) have held up better than those based on demand-side adoption (new tiers, new interfaces).

    The demand backdrop is not in question. IDC raised its 2026 AI infrastructure forecast to US$497 billion and expects spending to pass US$1 trillion in 202946. Storage is a small share of that, so the issue is not whether storage demand grows. It is which tiers and which business models capture it.

    That history sets the calibration here. Capacity trends get High. New tiers get High only where a dominant platform owner (NVIDIA with Dynamo and STX) is standardizing them. Tiers that need a whole ecosystem to move (CXL pooling, PLC, DNA) get Medium or Low. The 2026 NAND shortage is a reminder that the cycle can override the roadmap for two years at a time (Chapter 48).

    Conversation playbook¶

    Questions for a PM

    1. "What has to be true for a 600 TB DFM to ship before commodity 512 TB SSDs?" Strong: names the NAND generation and die capacity, the rebuild design and the qualification lead time. It says whether the gap to commodity drives is widening or narrowing.
    2. "In a sovereign AI deal, what do we lose on today?" Strong: names specifics such as local support, air-gap, certification or a domestic partner, and which competitor wins (often Dell, Huawei or DDN).
    3. "How does PureKVA make money if NVIDIA's CMX becomes the default context tier?" Strong: positions PureKVA as the G4 shared tier behind CMX, or as a CMX-compatible software layer, and gives a cost-per-token comparison, not a TTFT multiplier.

    Questions for an engineer

    1. "At 1 PB per module, what is the rebuild time and the exposure window to a second failure?" Strong: gives aggregate rebuild GB/s, the erasure-code geometry and the probability of data loss during rebuild.
    2. "Which parts of Purity could run on a BlueField-4 DPU, and what breaks?" Strong: separates data path from metadata and global deduplication, and names memory and core limits.
    3. "What does PLC do to our write path and endurance budget?" Strong: quantifies P/E cycles, read-retry latency and how much log structuring and wear leveling absorb.

    Don't say this:

    • "Disk is dead." HAMR order books run through 2028 and flash costs about 18× more per TB in 2026.
    • "CXL will replace storage tiers." CXL memory is DRAM at DRAM prices; it is a memory tier.
    • "DNA storage is coming soon." No production system exists, and write throughput needs orders-of-magnitude gains.
    • "1,000-layer NAND means 3× cheaper flash." Cost per bit depends on bonding and process steps, not layer count.
    • "Our KV cache gives 20× faster TTFT." Without the baseline and cost per token, that number persuades no one.

    Key terms¶

    • Context memory — storage tier holding inference KV cache between GPU memory and shared storage.
    • CMX / STX — NVIDIA's BlueField-4 context memory storage platform and storage reference architecture.
    • Co-packaged optics (CPO) — optical engines packaged next to a switch or processor die instead of pluggable modules.
    • HAMR — heat-assisted magnetic recording, which lets disks write smaller bits.
    • PLC — penta-level cell, 5 bits per NAND cell.
    • Sovereign AI — AI infrastructure built to keep data, control and operations within a jurisdiction.
    • Technology radar — chart placing trends by maturity ring and category.
