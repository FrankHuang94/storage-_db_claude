- **Site power cap** — The fixed grid allocation, in megawatts, that bounds all IT load a datacenter campus can run. | 38
- **Power-limited datacenter** — A facility whose output is bounded by available electricity rather than by chip supply or floor space. | 38
- **System-level TB/W** — Capacity per watt measured for a whole storage system including controllers, fans and networking, rather than for a bare drive. | 38
- **PB per rack** — Raw or usable petabytes held in one rack, limited in practice by rack power, cooling and floor loading. | 38
- **GB/s per kW** — Storage bandwidth delivered per kilowatt of power, an efficiency metric suited to AI workloads. | 38
- **Bandwidth per TB** — The throughput a device or system can deliver per terabyte stored, which falls as hard drives grow larger. | 38
- **JBOD** — "Just a bunch of disks": a drive enclosure with no storage controller logic, attached to a separate head server. | 38
- **Head server** — The server that runs storage software in front of one or more JBOD enclosures. | 38
- **Embodied carbon** — Greenhouse-gas emissions from manufacturing and shipping hardware, as distinct from emissions from powering it. | 38
- **Operational carbon** — Emissions from the electricity a system consumes over its service life. | 38
- **Dennard scaling** — The historical rule that transistor power density stayed constant as transistors shrank, whose end around 2005 made chips power-limited. | 38
- **Powered shell** — A datacenter building with grid power and cooling in place, ready for IT equipment to be installed. | 38
- **Floor loading** — The weight per unit area a datacenter floor can bear, a limit on densely packed disk racks. | 38
- **Nameplate power** — The rated maximum power of a device or power supply, usually well above its typical draw. | 38
