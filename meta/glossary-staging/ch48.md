- **Contract price** — the negotiated quarterly or monthly price that large buyers pay NAND or DRAM makers, as distinct from the more volatile spot price. | 48
- **Spot price** — the price for memory traded in small, immediate lots outside long-term contracts, usually the first to move in a cycle. | 48
- **Long-term agreement (LTA)** — a multi-year memory supply contract, increasingly with prepayments and price floors and ceilings. | 48
- **Price collar** — a contract term that sets both a floor and a ceiling on the price paid, limiting both upside and downside. | 48
- **Pass-through lag** — the delay between a change in component cost and the matching change in a vendor's selling prices. | 48
- **Revenue inflation** — revenue growth driven by higher selling prices rather than more units shipped. | 48
- **Strategic purchase** — buying components well ahead of need to secure supply and lock in cost, at the expense of near-term cash flow. | 48
- **Prepayment (supplier)** — cash paid to a component maker before delivery to secure allocation, recorded as a prepaid asset rather than inventory. | 48
- **Bit share** — a supplier's share of total NAND capacity shipped, as opposed to its share of revenue. | 48
- **Capex discipline** — a memory maker's restraint in adding wafer capacity, used to keep supply tight and prices firm. | 48
- **Nearline HDD** — high-capacity 7,200 rpm hard drives used for warm and cold data in data centres. | 48
- **HAMR** — heat-assisted magnetic recording, which briefly heats the platter with a laser so smaller, more stable magnetic grains can be written, raising HDD density. | 48
- **UltraSMR** — Western Digital's shingled magnetic recording technology, which overlaps tracks to raise HDD capacity at the cost of slower random writes. | 48
- **Entity List** — the US Commerce Department list of foreign firms that need a licence to receive US-origin technology; YMTC is on it. | 48
- **Effective $/TB** — the price per terabyte a customer can actually store after data reduction, rather than per raw terabyte. | 48
