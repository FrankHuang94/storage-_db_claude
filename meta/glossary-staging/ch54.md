- **Value stack** — The layers of an industry ranked by where profit is captured, rather than by the technical path an I/O takes. | 54
- **Build / buy / partner** — The three ways a company can obtain a capability: develop it in-house, acquire it, or contract for it with another firm. | 54
- **Tuck-in acquisition** — A small purchase of a team or product that is absorbed into an existing platform rather than run as a separate business. | 54
- **Strategic fit** — Whether an option strengthens a layer customers pay for, or protects one they do pay for. | 54
- **Technical fit** — Whether an acquired or partnered asset can run on, or beside, the existing operating system and control plane without adding a second data path. | 54
- **Second-OS problem** — The engineering cost and customer confusion of running two storage operating systems after an acquisition. | 54
- **Neutrality** — A vendor's ability to serve data stored on rivals' systems, which partners and multi-vendor buyers value. | 54
- **ARR multiple** — A purchase price or valuation divided by the target's annual recurring revenue. | 54
- **Payback period** — The years of incremental gross profit or cash flow needed to recover a purchase price. | 54
- **Antitrust remedy** — A divestiture or behavioural commitment that regulators require before clearing a deal. | 54
- **Foreign-investment review** — Government screening of cross-border acquisitions on national-security grounds, such as CFIUS in the United States. | 54
- **DSPM (data security posture management)** — Software that finds sensitive data across systems and checks who can access it. | 54
- **Do-nothing option** — Continuing the current plan without new acquisitions or major partnerships, evaluated as a real alternative rather than a default. | 54
